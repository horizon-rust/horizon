# Horizon dev vs vanilla 26.3 on Linux (Omarchy, Wayland): Ryzen 5 3550H + GTX 1050 (2026-10-30)

The first Linux run of [tools/perf_bench.py](../../tools/perf_bench.py) ([docs/benchmark.md](../../docs/benchmark.md)): Horizon,
vanilla 26.3 and vanilla with performance mods, render distances 8 and 16, 1280x720, three games each, every game from
a fresh world. The reports are the `*-linux-3550h-1050-rd*.json` files next to this page. RD 32 was left out: this
laptop's GTX 1050 has 3 GB of VRAM, and the docs' default distances put RD 32 outside its comfort zone.

```sh
python3 tools/perf_bench.py --table horizon-linux-3550h-1050 vanilla-linux-3550h-1050 vanilla-mods-linux-3550h-1050
```

## Setup

- **Machine:** AMD Ryzen 5 3550H (4 cores, 8 threads, boost 3.7 GHz), 7.7 GB RAM, NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 (3 GB,
  driver 580.178.04, Vulkan). Omarchy (Arch, kernel 7.2.8-9-omarchy), Wayland (compositor's session kept unlocked and
  the game windows on the real display), one 1920x1080 60 Hz screen, battery "Full", on mains. Laptop, NVMe storage.
  Named processes from the agent that drove the runs were the machine's only background load.
- **Horizon:** built from source at main commit `47a3b39fa9e4` (development build reporting app version 0.1.0),
  `target/release/horizon-client`, resources prepared from the player's own 26.3 install. Renders on the GTX 1050,
  Vulkan (wgpu); wgpu's `HighPerformance` pick lands on the discrete GPU and no adapter option exists.
- **Vanilla:** the 26.3 client jar with Fabric Loader 0.19.5 and the driver mod, Java 25.0.1 (Microsoft OpenJDK
  runtime), `-Xmx2G` with the official launcher's G1 flags and the version manifest's `-XX:StackShadowPages=32`,
  desktop at 1280x720.
- **Vanilla + mods:** the same with Sodium mc26.3-0.9.2, Lithium mc26.3-0.26.2, FerriteCore 9.0.0, ImmediatelyFast
  1.17.1 and Fabric API 0.161.0+26.3 (`tools/benchmark-deps/mods.lock.json`).
- **Same GPU.** Vanilla's LWJGL otherwise creates its context on the AMD Radeon Vega 8 of this laptop's iGPU
  (Mesa/radeonsi, OpenGL) — a different GPU than Horizon's, which is not a comparable comparison. The vanilla games
  were therefore pinned to the GTX 1050 with
  `__EGL_VENDOR_LIBRARY_FILENAMES=/usr/share/glvnd/egl_vendor.d/10_nvidia.json` and `__GLX_VENDOR_LIBRARY_NAME=nvidia`;
  with the pin on, vanilla 26.3 renders through its Vulkan backend on the NVIDIA driver (recorded per report, along
  with Horizon's).
- **GPU memory** reads empty for both games here: no per-process figure was obtained through `nvidia-smi` under this
  driver/compositor combination, and the table's footprint column stays without values (RSS is sampled from the
  process as on other pages).

## Load

Every game ran on an otherwise idle machine. A driver script waited for the 1-minute load average to fall under 1.0
before starting each game (checked every 10 s; it never had to wait long — the launch builds the agent made earlier
in the day had long cooled by the run window), then started it. The `load_avg_1m` field recorded before each game is
0.71–0.99 across all six combinations. Two starts during the day hit a driver-level SIGSEGV in `libEGL_nvidia.so.0+0xb660e`
(NVIDIA 580.178.04, proprietary stack, not symbolized): one vanilla game 19 s in at the title screen, one
vanilla + mods game mid-flight in the RD 8 forest pass, in different phases with different surrounding load each
time. The same games passed when rerun later, so neither crash is deterministic; no Horizon game ever hit it. The
crashed runs were discarded and rerun; every run whose numbers stand in this page (a) started with the load average
under 1.0 and (b) completed all its scenes.

## What it shows

- Horizon's average FPS is above vanilla's in 6 of the 8 combinations, by 2–83 %; the exceptions are the mountain,
  the one scene where vanilla averages above Horizon at both distances (RD 8: 634 ±16 vs 553 ±37; RD 16: 341 ±41 vs
  259 ±260 — that interval is wider than the difference, so read it as noise). Its 1% lows are above vanilla's in
  every combination, 1.2–3.5 times, and its 0.1% lows 2.0–4.7 times. The village is the widest lead
  (RD 8 average 566 vs 310, 1% low 171 vs 49, 0.1% low 118 vs 25; RD 16 average 369 vs 208, 1% low 107 vs 33).
- The performance mods leave vanilla's averages level or lower here — below vanilla in 7 of the 8 combinations
  (village RD 8: 200 vs 310) — and raise its 1% lows in only 1 of 8 (RD 16 forest, 71 vs 32). On this laptop Sodium
  does not rescue vanilla's lows the way it does on the Windows 9800X3D results: vanilla's weakness across these
  scenes is not the mesher Horizon replaces, but something else.
- Horizon reaches the title screen 5.4–7.7 times faster than vanilla (7.2–7.9 times faster than the modded client)
  and is in-world 4.5–5.9 times faster. It loads a scene's chunks 2.3–3.3 times faster. Its steady RSS is 52–54 %
  smaller than vanilla's.
- The GPU-footprint columns are empty here (see Setup) — the only figure this page can contribute to that debate is
  RSS, not VRAM.
